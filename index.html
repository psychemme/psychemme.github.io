PsycheMME: A Psychology-Informed Benchmarkfor Social Understanding and Interaction in Videos

Psychology-informed video understanding and interaction

PsycheMME evaluates how video models infer people’s states and needs, choose a response, and support that choice with evidence.

The benchmark contains 1,000 questions from 630 video episodes across eight psychology-informed capabilities.

Code Dataset Models Evaluation Logs

01 / Overview

Reading a situation, deciding how to respond

Recognizing a gesture, inferring a need and choosing a response are different judgments. PsycheMME evaluates them separately, with relevant history and social boundaries included where they matter.

A closer look at the benchmark Composition & statistics

630 episodes across eight source groups support 890 choice questions and 110 open questions. The overview illustrates 16 scene categories. Uneven capability counts motivate macro averaging across the eight capabilities.

Finding 01 / Timing

Interaction Timing remains challenging.

Timing has the lowest Verified score and credit retention for all 17 frozen models. Among correct single-choice responses, weak or contradicted cue support occurs in 49.74% of Timing responses, versus 7.62% for the other capabilities.

Finding 02 / Completeness

Similar Need scores conceal completeness differences.

Gemini 3.6 Flash and Claude Opus 5 have similar Need Verified scores (69.97 / 69.10). On the same 35 open Need questions, their full-credit rates are 28.57% / 68.57%, revealing a difference in answer completeness.

Finding 03 / Consensus

Voting can override correct minority answers.

For the eight proprietary models on 72 Timing questions, oracle answer coverage reaches 91.67%, while plurality voting reaches 57.64% and the best individual model reaches 66.67%. Shared wrong choices can outvote correct minority answers.

Finding 04 / Context

Performance depends on relevant context.

Full inputs improve Raw scores over Question-only by 12.50 points for Qwen3.5-9B and 12.43 for MiniCPM-V4.5. In the Memory intervention, gains depend on matched, relevant history.

03 / Methodology

From video selection to verified scores

Questions are generated, answered independently and reviewed by experts before the benchmark is frozen. Evaluation then scores the answer and the evidence given for it.

A /

Capability taxonomy

Social Information Processing and the Perception-Action Cycle guide the eight capabilities: what can be observed, what is inferred about a person, and how to respond.

B /

Benchmark construction

Video curation combines six public datasets with public vlogs, interviews, and daily-life recordings. Human screening assisted by VLMs selects episodes with observable social cues and adequate visual quality.

01 / Question Agent

Propose a grounded question.

Generate a question, candidate answer, options where applicable, and supporting cues, guided by the target capability.

02 / Answer Agent

Answer independently.

Re-examine the video using the question, options, and public context. The proposed answer, private cues, and rationale are withheld.

03 / Critic Agent

Check both reasoning paths.

Review answerability, cue support, and logical coherence. Unresolved candidates are discarded after at most two refinement rounds.

Five independent reviewers.Researchers with psychology backgrounds check validity, correctness, and psychological consistency. Only unanimously approved items enter the frozen benchmark.

C /

Reasoning-Verified evaluation.

Raw Answer

Choice answers must exactly match the reference option or complete option set. A fixed semantic Judge assigns open answers zero, partial (0.5), or full credit.

Reasoning-Verified

Weight answer credit by the presence of an informative rationale, support from annotated reference cues, and logical integrity. Qwen3.8-27B serves as the fixed Judge; reference answers enter scoring only.

Answer correctnessInformative rationaleEvidence SupportLogical Integrity

Both headline metrics are macro-averaged across the eight capabilities. Evaluation concerns offline judgments in recorded interactions.

Section 3.5 / The scoring rule
si = ai ci ei ℓi / λ2

λ = 1.2. Answer correctness ai ∈ [0, 1]; rationale coverage ci ∈ {0, 1}; cue support ei and logical integrity ℓi ∈ [0, λ].

With a correct answer, an informative rationale, normal support, and acceptable logic, credit is 1 / 1.44 ≈ 69.44%. The Raw–Verified gap includes this fixed normalization; it cannot be attributed entirely to reasoning failure.
